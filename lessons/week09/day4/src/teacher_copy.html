Entering Night: Moishe’s Warning and the Symbol of Night

Night by Elie Wiesel, pp. 3–22 (print file: PDF pages 18–36) · Standards 8.T.C.2.c · 8.T.T.1.a · 8.T.SS.2.a · 8.T.SS.1.d
Essential questionWhat does the community’s reaction to the warning reveal about human nature, and what might Wiesel’s repeated word “night” come to symbolize?
Learning targetI can analyze how the people of Sighet respond to warnings and how Wiesel begins to use the word “night” as a symbol, and use evidence from the text to explain what those patterns reveal.
The distinction · post it, keep it up all periodWARNING → REACTION → INFERENCE. The warning is what the town is told. The reaction is what they do with it. The inference is what that reveals about people. And for night: LITERAL → PATTERN → SYMBOL. The sorting question all period: “Are you telling me what happened, or what it reveals?”
Homework = first read · class = rereadScholars read pp. 3–22 last night with the Opening Reading Check. Today is not a second first-read. Class reconstructs the plot fast, rereads the moment that matters (Moishe, pp. 5–7), and spends the time on inference, symbolism, discussion, and writing.

NIGHT · pp. 3–22
Warning Reactions key
M · pp. 6–7Moishe’s eyewitness account. They refuse to believe or listen; call him mad (p. 7).People may doubt the messenger rather than accept news too terrible to imagine. Disbelief protects their normal life.
2 · pp. 8–9Fascists seize power; German troops enter Hungary; the Budapest report. “Yet we still were not worried… it was all in the abstract” (p. 9). “Optimism soon revived” (p. 9).People keep danger “abstract” until it touches them. Hope can become a way of not looking.
3 · pp. 9–12German soldiers arrive; the officer brings chocolates (p. 10). Leaders arrested; Moishe: “I warned you” (p. 10). “It’s not lethal…” (p. 11). The ghetto “ruled by delusion” (p. 12).People adjust to each new step and call it normal. Small kindnesses and gradual change make it easier to deny the danger.
Night Tracker key · do not post these
p. 12“Night fell.” Father summoned; the story “would remain unfinished.”The ordinary evening breaks; the start of the end of safety.
p. 14Someone knocked “that night”: “we might still have been able to flee.”A last warning lost; too late; escape cut off.
p. 18“Night had fallen.” “It was to be the last night spent in our house.”Losing home; endings.
p. 21“NIGHT. No one was praying for the night to pass quickly.” Stars as sparks of a fire “consuming us.”Destruction; fear; hope fading.
p. 22The last family meal; “I spent that night going over memories.”The last of family life; sleeplessness, dread.

Stretch press: on p. 3 night meant running to the synagogue to pray (“by night I would run to the synagogue”). What changes about night by p. 21?

Grounding · night on p. 12

What is happening: An ordinary evening in the ghetto; neighbors in the courtyard; Elie’s father is telling stories. A policeman calls him to an emergency Council meeting. He comes back near midnight with one word: “Transports” (p. 13).

Why it matters: “Night fell” is the first time in the reading that night arrives with the danger. Early on, night meant prayer and study (pp. 3, 8). That change is the pattern scholars will find.

Say while doing
